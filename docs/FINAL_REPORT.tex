We selected histogram gradient boosting implemented in NumPy, combining
Phase 18 diet representation, Phase 24 restricted imputation and 256 candidate
features per node. The team reported \textbf{AIcrowd F1 0.455 and accuracy
0.883}. This final pipeline has 297 inputs. Its threshold-calibration F1 is
0.444071; an independent nested evaluation of this exact combination was not run.

\heading{Data and evaluation}
The dataset contains 328,135 labelled rows, 321 predictors and 109,379 test
rows. The target \code{\_MICHD} records self-reported myocardial infarction
or coronary heart disease. Positives make up 8.83\% of training labels;
44.79\% of predictor cells are blank. Always predicting negative gives
91.17\% accuracy and F1 zero, motivating our focus on F1.

The initial stratified split (seed 20260918) gives 262,508 development and
65,627 local validation rows. Historical comparisons use three outer folds
on development data (seed 20260919). Two inner folds within each outer
training partition generate out-of-fold (OOF) predictions and select the
F1 threshold; that threshold is applied to the excluded outer fold.
Preprocessing, imputation, feature selection and sampling use only the
training partition of each fit. Validation preserves all rows and prevalence.
Later submission candidates instead calibrate thresholds on three OOF folds
covering all labelled rows; those calibration scores are reported separately.

\heading{Methods and preprocessing}
In \code{implementations.py}, we implemented the six required methods:
linear regression with GD/SGD, least squares, ridge, logistic regression and
L2 logistic regression. Least squares and ridge solve normal equations;
linear losses use half-MSE and returned losses exclude regularisation.
Historical linear baselines use median imputation, training-fitted scaling
and age interactions. Logistic regression uses $\gamma=0.2$, 4,000 updates
and $\lambda=0$; ridge uses $\lambda=10^{-4}$ and inner-OOF logistic calibration.

\textbf{Codebook cleaning.} The CDC 2015 codebook guides types, units,
response codes and exclusions. Removing identifiers, interview date,
survey-design fields and redundant measurements leaves 295 source columns.
Documented nonresponse codes become \code{NaN}; zero-quantity codes such as
\code{ALCDAY5=888} and \code{PHYSHLTH=88} become zero. On retained development
columns this recodes 1.10 million finite nonresponses and about 900,000
zero quantities. Histogram trees fit quantile bins and missing-value
directions on training rows, then use logistic gradients and curvatures
to update leaf predictions.

\textbf{Final representation.} Phase 18 removes six mixed-unit raw diet
columns while retaining their existing CDC per-day equivalents, leaving 289
source columns. Phase 24 adds eight original missing-reason indicators and
feature-only imputation: Naive Bayes fills documented unknown/refused responses
in \code{INCOME2}, \code{PNEUVAC3} and \code{EMPLOY1}; ridge predicts missing
height/weight and BMI is reconstructed when possible. Imputers and anchor
bins are fitted within each training partition. Structural blanks remain
missing. The combined representation has \textbf{297 columns}.

The selected model uses \textbf{200 trees, depth 5}, learning rate 0.05,
at least 200 rows per leaf, 64 quantile bins, \textbf{256 candidates per node},
leaf L2 of 1, positive weight 1 and model seed 20260920. It retains the
historical Phase 14 numerical implementation for exact reproduction.

limitations, other diagnoses, or both. They give calibration F1

\heading{Interpretation and limitations}
\textbf{Split-search audit.} Phase 34 adds an omitted candidate separating
all observed values from missing responses. With Phase 14 features and
parameters fixed, nested F1 changes from 0.442155 to 0.440803: 206 more
true positives and 907 more false positives. Its paired conditional 95\%
interval [$-0.00300$, $+0.00032$] includes zero. The final submission keeps
the historical search; results for it and the corrected algorithm remain
distinct. These intervals condition on fixed fitted models and thresholds.

\textbf{Disease timing.} \code{HAREHAB1} asks about rehabilitation after a
heart attack; all 620 recorded development responses belong to positives.
Removing it from historical Phase 14 lowers F1 by 0.01112. It remains in
the final model to classify reported disease. A model intended to predict
before an event would require excluding it and auditing the timing of other
responses. The score does not establish prospective clinical performance.

\textbf{Repeated selection.} Development folds were reused for many choices,
and initial baselines consulted the local 20\% partition. Later candidates
use all labelled rows for calibration; AIcrowd feedback contributed to
selection. Neither calibration F1 nor the selected leaderboard score is an
untouched estimate of performance after this search. The historical nested
results do not measure the exact combined submission.

\heading{Reproduction and final output}
Install the dependencies, supply the official CSVs and run
\code{python -m tools.prepare\_data}. The selected implementation,
preprocessing and threshold are fixed in \path{configs/final_model.json}.
Train on all labelled rows and generate predictions with:\par
{\small\ttfamily python run.py -{}-data-dir data/raw/dataset -{}-output submission.csv}\par
The selected threshold is \textbf{0.22007772525099117}. The verified CSV has
109,379 rows: \textbf{13,895 positive and 95,484 negative}, header
\code{Id,Prediction}, labels $-1/+1$ and IDs in official sample order.
Its SHA-256 starts \code{d01c3949dea567dc}. Compact manifests, verification
records and reported feedback are retained under \path{results/};
\path{docs/FINAL_REPORT.md} links the full evidence and calibration commands.
